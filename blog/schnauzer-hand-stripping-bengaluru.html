Schnauzer Hand Stripping vs Clipping

What a hand-stripped Schnauzer coat feels like

Put your hand on the back of a hand-stripped Schnauzer. It feels harsh and tight. It springs back under your palm, like a good brush. That isn't a groomer's finish. That is the coat the breed was born with. I knew all of this before I ever touched Prada. I had been following hand stripping online for a while. A Schnauzer has two coats, a soft one underneath and a wiry one on top. The wiry hairs grow, die, loosen, and then just sit there. They don't fall out on their own. But knowing it on a screen and feeling it under your fingers are two different things. Hand stripping is simply taking those dead hairs out. Gently, when they are ready. New hard hair grows in behind them. A relaxed dog hardly notices. Clipping cuts everything. Dead hair, living hair, all of it, halfway down. It's quicker. It's what most groomers do. And for a while, it looks fine.

Why a clipped Schnauzer's coat goes soft and grey

Give it a few years and the coat changes. Why??
Because clipping cuts off the hard tip of every wire hair. The dead hair stays in. Nothing pulls it out, so no fresh hard hair replaces it. Meanwhile the soft undercoat underneath keeps renewing itself, the way it always has. So the balance tips. Fewer fresh wire hairs on top. More soft undercoat filling the gaps. Do that every few weeks for a few years, and the coat is mostly undercoat. Soft. Fluffy. And salt and pepper fades to a flat grey, because the clipper cuts straight through the colour bands on every hair.
Is your Schnauzer softer than she was as a puppy? Lighter? That's usually why.

Is hand stripping better for a Schnauzer's skin?

Honestly? I don't know. Nobody has studied it in Schnauzers. What I can tell you is why I do it, and to me it makes sense. When we clip, the dead wire hairs stay in. They sit in the follicle, crowding it, while the new hard hair struggles to come through. The soft undercoat keeps growing around them. Does that clog the skin and cause bumps? Some groomers think so. I don't know. Science tells us a dead hair can sit in its follicle after it has let go of the root. Groomers say wire hairs stay there until something takes them out. Studies in other breeds show clipping
can change the coat and the skin. So my logic is simple. Take out what is dead. Let the coat shed the way it was built to.

Why the Schnauzer cut looks the way it does

There is a reason the breed wears this coat. Schnauzers were farm dogs in southern Germany. They guarded yards. They ran with carriages. They kept the stables free of rats. Breeders will tell you the beard, the eyebrows and that hard coat protected them from bites. Nobody has proved it. But picture a small dog grabbing a rat that bites back, and it makes sense to me. Some of the cut is that history. Some of it is the show ring. The long skirt and those heavy, full legs came much later, mostly from American show fashion. The early German dogs had far less hair on their legs. Here in Bengaluru, a long skirt mostly collects mud, ticks and monsoon damp. So I keep it lighter. Unless you really love it.

Can a clipped Schnauzer be hand stripped again?

Most pet Schnauzers in Bengaluru have been clipped, often for years. So when their owners hear all this, they usually ask me one thing.
My Schnauzer has been clipped for years. Is it too late?
Sometimes. Sometimes not. I won't promise you a show coat. Even breed specialists say bringing a clipped coat back is the exception. And some Schnauzers just have soft coats. They were never going to strip. So we test a small patch first. Is there still wire in there? Does the dead hair let go? If it does, we bring it back slowly. Shorter rolling visits. A little coat each time, so the hard hair can find its way through. Months, not one appointment. If it doesn't, we don't force it. We give your dog a breed-specific haircut, the classic Schnauzer pattern done with clippers on the body and scissors on the legs, beard and eyebrows. Then we card out the dead undercoat, so the coat stays as healthy and as true in colour as it can.

Should you pluck a Schnauzer's ears?

People ask me why we don't pluck Schnauzer ears. Logic. If you pull the hair out, you have hurt the skin inside the ear. You have opened up the follicle. And there are already bacteria and yeast living in there, quietly, doing no harm. Why would I give them a way in? So we trim. Just at the opening of the ear canal, never inside it. A little air moves. The ear stays drier. Nothing gets torn. Not every vet agrees with me, and that's fine. If your vet wants your dog's ears plucked for a medical reason, listen to your vet. Tell us, and we will work with it.

Hand stripping without restraint

A full hand strip can take a couple of hours on the grooming table. No dog should be held still for that long. At Pawpad, the dog sets the pace. If there's a lot of coat, we split it over visits. A puppy on her first strip and an old dog with a soft clipped coat do not need the same day.

When you strip a coat by hand, your fingers go over every inch of the skin, so you notice things. Once in a while we find small blackheads along a Schnauzer's spine. Schnauzer bumps. Vets believe they are mostly inherited. We rarely see them. If we do, we will show you, and send you to your vet.

Schnauzer hand stripping: quick answers

Does hand stripping hurt a Schnauzer?
Not when the coat is ready and the dog is relaxed. Hand stripping only removes dead wire hairs that have already loosened from the root. At Pawpad the dog sets the pace, and we split the work over visits if there is a lot of coat.

How often should a Schnauzer be hand stripped?
It depends on the dog and the coat. Most hand-stripped Schnauzers need maintenance to keep the coat tight. We plan the schedule after we have seen your dog's coat.

Can a clipped Schnauzer go back to hand stripping?
Sometimes. We test a small patch first. If there is still wire in the coat, we bring it back over several shorter rolling visits. If not, we give your dog a breed-specific haircut and card out the dead undercoat.

Why has my Schnauzer's coat gone soft?
Repeated clipping cuts off the hard tips of the wire hairs and leaves the dead hair in. The soft undercoat keeps renewing itself, so over a few years it takes over the top of the coat. Salt and pepper coats also fade to grey.
